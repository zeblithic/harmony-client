## Harmony v0.2.3 — files, governance, a steadier network, a way back in, and the rough edges sanded down

The second Harmony alpha you can actually install, and a big one: ~five weeks and 200+ changes
since v0.1.2. You can now share encrypted files with specific people, run real votes in your
community, pick your voice devices mid-call, stay connected even when the network gets rough,
get yourself un-stuck if the app ever fails to start — and, new in this build, keep your sidebar
organized the way you left it and get a calmer first launch on macOS.

### ⚠️ Everyone needs to update

This release changes the peer-to-peer networking layer (iroh 0.98 → 1.0), and **the two versions
cannot talk to each other**. A v0.1.2 client and a v0.2.3 client will not connect, no matter how
healthy both networks look.

If you have Harmony installed, let the auto-updater run, or reinstall from the links below.
If half your community updates and half doesn't, the two halves will silently stop seeing
each other — so it's worth nudging the stragglers.

### What's new

**Your sidebar stays the way you left it** *(new in this build)*
- Collapse a community in the sidebar to tidy up your list and it now **stays collapsed**.
  Previously, collapsing a community was quietly undone the moment *any* community received an
  update — a new message, a mention — silently re-expanding everything you'd just tucked away.
- Your collapse/expand choices persist across those background updates now, and so do each
  folder's display mode and sort order. If you're in a lot of communities with a lot of channels,
  the sidebar is finally yours to organize.

**A calmer first launch on macOS** *(new in this build)*
- The macOS build is now ad-hoc sealed, so on first launch Gatekeeper should show the ordinary
  "unidentified developer" prompt instead of the alarming **"Harmony is damaged and can't be
  opened."** It's the same one-time approve either way — the app was never actually damaged — but
  the message no longer reads like the download broke.
- The build still isn't signed with an Apple Developer certificate (a deliberate, permanent alpha
  choice), so you'll still approve it once. The install docs now walk through **both** messages,
  including the one-line `xattr` fix if you ever land on the "damaged" wording.

**A way back in when startup gets stuck**
- If Harmony ever fails to start and lands on the **"Couldn't start Harmony"** screen — and
  retrying just keeps failing — you're no longer at a dead end. A quiet **Still stuck?** link
  now reveals two ways out:
  - **Restore from your recovery phrase** — bring this device's identity back in place.
  - **Reset this device & start fresh** — this first backs up your current identity to a folder
    on this device, then clears it so the app can start over into first-run setup. It asks you
    to confirm, and tells you plainly what you'll lose without your recovery phrase.
- If the specific problem is that this device is no longer part of your identity, you'll get a
  dedicated screen that leads with **"Your data on your other devices is safe"** — with the same
  restore-or-reset options right there.
- These particular startup failures are unlikely on a fresh install — they surfaced on machines
  also used for development — but the point is that no one should ever be permanently locked out
  with no way to recover, even on an alpha build.

**Share files, encrypted, with the people you choose**
- Share a file with specific **friends** — they get it, nobody else can read it. Sharing runs over
  the friend connection, so being in the same community isn't enough on its own; add someone as a
  friend first.
- Everything shared with you shows up in **Shared with me**, ready to download.
- Large files now stream as they encrypt, so big attachments no longer block the app.
- Changed your mind? Revoking access pushes the revocation out to everyone who had it.

**Communities can actually govern themselves**
- **Proposals and voting** are live: conviction voting, with results that execute themselves
  when they pass — no admin needs to be awake to apply the outcome.
- **Set your own thresholds** per community for who can invite, kick, and change power levels.
- **Admin recovery**: if the people holding admin vanish, members you designated ahead of time
  can recover the community — after a waiting period, and any admin can veto during it. Losing
  your admins no longer means losing the community.

**Voice and calls**
- Pick your microphone and speaker, and switch either one mid-call without dropping.
- Missed a call? It's in your history now, with who called and when.

**Channels**
- **@-mentions** with autocomplete as you type.
- See who's around: member counts, online dots across all your communities, and an
  invisible mode when you'd rather not show up.

**Staying connected — the quiet half of this release**
- Reconnection is no longer one dial and a shrug: there's a real per-peer retry ladder that
  keeps working the problem.
- Rejoining after time offline catches you up on what you missed, efficiently — it works out
  precisely which messages you're missing instead of refetching the channel.
- macOS launches promptly again (a system network call could previously stall startup).
- Leaving a community now actually reclaims its disk space.
- **Community sync no longer stalls out.** A boot-time peer outage used to be able to wedge
  syncing permanently — in either direction — while the node still reported itself perfectly
  healthy. It recovers on its own now.
- **The community relay keeps serving.** A relay volunteer could previously stop serving
  *everyone* while its process stayed alive, recoverable only by a restart; that's fixed, and
  the network can now actually report whether the relay is working.
- **Bigger communities hold together.** Community state used to silently stop publishing once a
  community's membership history crossed a size cap — every community would eventually hit it.
  State is now kept compact and bounded.
- **Network health tells the truth.** A peer that's gone quiet is shown as stale instead of
  "healthy" indefinitely, so the diagnostics reflect reality.
- **Friends — and the file sharing built on them — actually converge.** Adding a friend by key
  now completes on both sides, and you can see your own outbound request while it's pending.
- **Your votes survive a restart.** A proposal you can see before a restart is still there after.

### Fixed since v0.1.2

- **Display name and the backup reminder are now per-identity, not per-machine.** The v0.1.2
  known issue — where a second identity on the same machine inherited the first one's name and
  backup state — is fixed.
- **Onboarding tells the truth about where your key lives.** The first-run screen no longer
  claims your identity key is in the OS keychain when it's actually in the encrypted-file store.
- **Settings and toolbar controls are all reachable and honest.** Clipped tabs and toolbars now
  fit at the default window size, and the Settings gear opens Settings instead of silently doing
  nothing while claiming to be active.

### Known issues

- **Builds aren't signed with an Apple Developer / Windows code-signing certificate.** macOS
  Gatekeeper and Windows SmartScreen will still ask you to approve Harmony on first launch; the
  install docs walk through it. On macOS the app is now ad-hoc sealed so the prompt should be the
  ordinary "unidentified developer" one rather than "damaged" — but if you downloaded an older
  build, or still hit the "damaged" wording, the `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine` one-liner in
  the install docs clears it. Paid code-signing certificates are not planned for alpha.
- **Per-platform smoke-test status:** The bulk of this release is the same code a live two-node
  **Windows** fleet validated end-to-end for the v0.2.1 draft — invite-only join, two-way channel
  messaging, offline catch-up, encrypted per-friend file sharing with revocation, honest
  network-health reporting, and a sustained 45-minute connection soak all passed. The two fixes
  new in v0.2.3 (sidebar-state persistence, macOS ad-hoc sealing) and the startup-recovery escape
  hatch are covered by automated tests but were added after that live fleet run.

### What to test

- **Update from v0.1.2** — does the auto-updater find and install this release cleanly?
- **Sidebar organization** — collapse a few communities, then have another client (or another
  member) send a message into one of them; confirm your collapsed communities *stay* collapsed
  instead of springing back open. Set a folder's display mode and sort order and confirm those
  stick across updates too.
- **macOS first launch** — on a Mac that's never run Harmony, confirm the downloaded build opens
  with the ordinary unsigned-app prompt rather than "damaged".
- **Recovery escape hatch** — on a machine with a broken/absent identity, confirm the
  "Couldn't start Harmony" screen offers **Still stuck?**, that **Reset this device** backs up
  the old identity to a folder and drops you into first-run setup, and that **Restore from
  recovery phrase** brings the same identity back.
- **File sharing** — add a friend first (the picker is empty until you have one), then share with
  them, confirm a third member of the same community can't see it, then revoke and confirm it
  disappears. Try a large file.
- **Voting** — create a proposal, vote it through, confirm the outcome applies on its own, then
  restart and confirm the proposal is still there.
- **Voice** — switch microphone and speaker mid-call.
- **@-mentions** and presence in channels.
- **Go offline for a while, come back** — does your channel history fill in?
- First-run onboarding and identity backup, on a machine with no Harmony identity.
