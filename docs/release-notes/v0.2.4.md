## Harmony v0.2.4 — read receipts, and a network that shrugs off a bad clock

The Harmony alpha you can install, and the sturdiest cut yet: ~six weeks and 260+ changes
since v0.1.2. You can share encrypted files with specific people, run real votes in your
community, pick your voice devices mid-call, stay connected when the network gets rough, and
get yourself un-stuck if the app ever fails to start. New in this build: **opt-in read
receipts** for DMs, a large **robustness pass that stops one participant's wrong or malicious
clock from disrupting everyone else**, **invites that can't be double-spent or leave a phantom
member behind**, and contacts that **keep their faces and names** even offline.

### ⚠️ Everyone needs to update

This release changes the peer-to-peer networking layer (iroh 0.98 → 1.0), and **the two versions
cannot talk to each other**. A v0.1.2 client and a v0.2.4 client will not connect, no matter how
healthy both networks look.

If you have Harmony installed, let the auto-updater run, or reinstall from the links below.
If half your community updates and half doesn't, the two halves will silently stop seeing
each other — so it's worth nudging the stragglers.

### What's new

**Read receipts — but only if you want them** *(new in this build)*
- Read receipts are **opt-in and per-DM, off by default**. Turn them on for a specific
  conversation and the person you're talking to sees a **"Seen HH:MM"** marker; leave them off —
  the default — and nothing about when or whether you've read a message is ever shared.
- It's a per-conversation choice, so you decide case by case, and you can turn it back off at any
  time. Receipts travel over the same private channel as the DM itself — there's no broadcast and
  no third party in the middle.

**A network that shrugs off a bad clock** *(new in this build)*
- A large, mostly-invisible hardening pass: Harmony no longer takes another device's clock at
  face value. A participant whose clock is wrong — accidentally or deliberately — can no longer
  stall your community's votes, forge or roll back a membership change, poison a poll result, or
  shove stale entries to the top of discovery.
- Timestamps that arrive from other devices are now bounded and sanity-checked at **every** trust
  boundary — governance actions, voting, membership, community mint records, stored content, peer
  discovery, and profile cards. The protections hold whether the bad clock is someone else's or
  your own drifting one.
- Practically: shared voice sessions and community governance stay coherent even when someone on
  the call has a misconfigured clock, and a whole class of "lie about what time it is" attacks is
  closed.

**Invites you can trust exactly once** *(new in this build)*
- A single-use invite is now genuinely single-use and atomic — it can't be double-claimed in a
  race, and a join that fails partway through no longer burns the invite or records a **phantom
  member** who never actually arrived. A failed redemption rolls back cleanly.
- The host now verifies a community's admin bootstrap before publishing an invite, so a malformed
  or tampered invite is caught before it goes out.
- After you redeem an invite, the app shows a clear **"catching up"** state while the community
  syncs in, instead of looking empty or stuck.

**Your contacts keep their faces and names** *(new in this build)*
- Avatars and display names are cached locally now, so people you know still show up with their
  picture and name when you're **offline or right after a restart** — no more blank avatars and
  hex addresses until the network catches back up. The name on a file someone shared with you
  resolves the same way.

**Smoother @-mentions** *(new in this build)*
- Mentioning someone in a channel now uses inline chips as you type, with autocomplete that finds
  people by name **or** by their identity address, and a sensible fallback when a name isn't known
  yet.

**Honest diagnostics and a cleaner reset** *(new in this build)*
- Network-health reporting got more truthful across the board — publish-retry backoff, sync
  staleness, and real per-community peer presence are surfaced accurately, so the in-app
  diagnostics reflect what's actually happening on the wire.
- Resetting your identity now does exactly what it says on the tin: a clean-slate reset really
  clears local data, and the recovery copy no longer over-promises what a reset keeps.

**Your sidebar stays the way you left it**
- Collapse a community in the sidebar and it **stays collapsed** — previously, any background
  update (a new message, a mention) quietly re-expanded everything you'd tucked away. Your
  collapse/expand choices, folder display modes, and sort orders all persist now.

**A calmer first launch on macOS**
- The macOS build is ad-hoc sealed, so first launch shows the ordinary **"unidentified developer"**
  prompt instead of the alarming **"Harmony is damaged and can't be opened."** Same one-time
  approve either way — the app was never actually damaged — but the message no longer reads like
  the download broke. The build still isn't signed with an Apple Developer certificate (a
  deliberate, permanent alpha choice), and the install docs walk through both messages, including
  the one-line `xattr` fix.

**A way back in when startup gets stuck**
- If Harmony ever fails to start and lands on **"Couldn't start Harmony"** — and retrying keeps
  failing — a quiet **Still stuck?** link now reveals two ways out:
  - **Restore from your recovery phrase** — bring this device's identity back in place.
  - **Reset this device & start fresh** — backs up your current identity to a folder on this
    device first, then clears it so the app can start over into first-run setup.
- If the specific problem is that this device is no longer part of your identity, a dedicated
  screen leads with **"Your data on your other devices is safe"** and offers the same options.
  These failures are unlikely on a fresh install, but no one should ever be permanently locked out.

**Share files, encrypted, with the people you choose**
- Share a file with specific **friends** — they get it, nobody else can read it. Sharing runs over
  the friend connection, so add someone as a friend first.
- Everything shared with you shows up in **Shared with me**, ready to download.
- Large files stream as they encrypt, so big attachments no longer block the app.
- Changed your mind? Revoking access pushes the revocation out to everyone who had it.

**Communities can actually govern themselves**
- **Proposals and voting** are live: conviction voting, with results that execute themselves when
  they pass — no admin needs to be awake to apply the outcome.
- **Set your own thresholds** per community for who can invite, kick, and change power levels.
- **Admin recovery**: if the people holding admin vanish, members you designated ahead of time can
  recover the community — after a waiting period, and any admin can veto during it.

**Voice and calls**
- Pick your microphone and speaker, and switch either one mid-call without dropping.
- Missed a call? It's in your history now, with who called and when.

**Channels and presence**
- See who's around: member counts, online dots across all your communities, and an invisible mode
  when you'd rather not show up.

**Staying connected — the quiet half of these releases**
- Reconnection is a real per-peer retry ladder now, not one dial and a shrug.
- Rejoining after time offline catches you up on exactly what you missed instead of refetching the
  whole channel.
- macOS launches promptly again (a system network call could previously stall startup).
- Leaving a community actually reclaims its disk space.
- **Community sync no longer stalls out.** A boot-time peer outage used to be able to wedge syncing
  permanently while the node still reported itself healthy. It recovers on its own now, and a
  self-healing watchdog keeps the community relay serving instead of silently going dark.
- **Bigger communities hold together.** Community state is kept compact and bounded, so a long
  membership history no longer silently stops a community from publishing.
- **Network health tells the truth.** A peer that's gone quiet is shown as stale rather than
  "healthy" indefinitely.
- **Friends — and the file sharing built on them — actually converge.** Adding a friend by key now
  completes on both sides, and you can see your own outbound request while it's pending.
- **Your votes survive a restart.** A proposal you can see before a restart is still there after.

### Fixed since v0.1.2

- **Display name and the backup reminder are now per-identity, not per-machine.** The v0.1.2 known
  issue — where a second identity on the same machine inherited the first one's name and backup
  state — is fixed.
- **Onboarding tells the truth about where your key lives.** The first-run screen no longer claims
  your identity key is in the OS keychain when it's actually in the encrypted-file store, and the
  underlying store now reports its ground truth rather than mere keychain availability.
- **Settings and toolbar controls are all reachable and honest.** Clipped tabs and toolbars fit at
  the default window size, and the Settings gear opens Settings instead of silently doing nothing.

### Known issues

- **Builds aren't signed with an Apple Developer / Windows code-signing certificate.** macOS
  Gatekeeper and Windows SmartScreen will still ask you to approve Harmony on first launch; the
  install docs walk through it. On macOS the app is ad-hoc sealed so the prompt should be the
  ordinary "unidentified developer" one rather than "damaged" — but if you downloaded an older
  build, or still hit the "damaged" wording, the `xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine` one-liner in the
  install docs clears it. Paid code-signing certificates are not planned for alpha.
- **Per-platform smoke-test status:** The core of this release is the same code a live two-node
  **Windows** fleet validated end-to-end for the v0.2.1 draft — invite-only join, two-way channel
  messaging, offline catch-up, encrypted per-friend file sharing with revocation, honest
  network-health reporting, and a sustained 45-minute connection soak all passed. Everything under
  **"New in this build"** (opt-in read receipts, the bounded-clock robustness pass, invite-redeem
  hardening, durable avatars/names, @-mention polish) landed after that live fleet run and is
  covered by automated tests but has not yet been through a live two-node smoke.

### What to test

- **Update from v0.1.2** — does the auto-updater find and install this release cleanly?
- **Read receipts** — in a DM, turn receipts on and confirm the other side shows "Seen HH:MM" once
  they've read up to your latest message; then confirm a DM left on the default (off) shares
  nothing, and that turning receipts back off stops them.
- **A wrong clock doesn't break things** — if you can, set one device's system clock well off (or
  use a machine that drifts) and confirm votes, invites, and membership still behave — the skewed
  device shouldn't be able to stall or corrupt shared state.
- **Invites are single-use** — redeem an invite, confirm the "catching up" state, and confirm the
  same invite can't be redeemed a second time or by two people at once.
- **Contacts keep their faces** — go offline (or restart) and confirm people you know still show
  their avatar and display name rather than a blank/hex placeholder.
- **Sidebar organization** — collapse a few communities, have another client send a message into
  one, and confirm your collapsed communities *stay* collapsed.
- **macOS first launch** — on a Mac that's never run Harmony, confirm the build opens with the
  ordinary unsigned-app prompt rather than "damaged".
- **Recovery escape hatch** — on a machine with a broken/absent identity, confirm "Couldn't start
  Harmony" offers **Still stuck?**, that **Reset** backs up + drops into first-run, and that
  **Restore** brings the same identity back.
- **File sharing** — add a friend first, share with them, confirm a third community member can't
  see it, then revoke and confirm it disappears. Try a large file.
- **Voting** — create a proposal, vote it through, confirm the outcome applies on its own, then
  restart and confirm the proposal is still there.
- **Voice** — switch microphone and speaker mid-call.
- **@-mentions** and presence in channels.
- **Go offline for a while, come back** — does your channel history fill in?
- First-run onboarding and identity backup, on a machine with no Harmony identity.
